# Season Plan 2026-27 — High School 100/200 Sprinter
## Double peak: mini peak Sat Feb 20, 2027 (indoor championships) · major peak Sat May 15, 2027 (outdoor championships)

Built for one high school 100/200 sprinter training inside a school week (after-school
practice Mon-Fri, optional Saturday), using the session library in
`track/sprinter-workout-database/` (codes like ACC-03, MAX-01 refer to sessions.json).

## What this borrows from top programs

- **Double periodization (the elite standard).** The research consensus on elite sprint
  programs (Haugen et al., 2019) is exactly this shape: preparation, indoor peak, second
  preparation, outdoor peak. One peak is not how top sprinters are built.
- **Charlie Francis (Canada) — short to long + high-low.** Acceleration first, max velocity
  next, speed endurance layered on last. High-CNS work (95%+ sprinting, heavy lifting,
  high-impact plyos) only on high days, always 48h+ apart, tempo (65-75%) on low days as
  recovery. His 10-day taper frames both peaks here.
- **Stephen Francis / MVP Track Club (Jamaica — Powell, Fraser-Pryce) — strength base and a
  ~30% volume cut across the last 10 days** before a major meet. The heavy strength phases
  and the taper math come from this camp.
- **Glen Mills / Racers TC (Jamaica — Bolt) — special endurance for the 200.** Long,
  fast-relaxed reps (the SPE sessions) so the last 50m of the 200 is trained, not survived.
- **Clyde Hart (Baylor) — tempo discipline.** Extensive tempo progresses all fall; "fast and
  relaxed" 200s (SPE-02) are Hart's signature borrowed for 200m support work.
- **Altis (Dan Pfaff) — technical quality over volume.** Every speed session has one technical
  target; when form breaks, the session ends. Speed is fed in small, perfect doses.
- **NCAA sprint powers (LSU — Shaver, Florida — Holloway) — the double-peak calendar is
  their season too** (indoor nationals, then outdoor nationals), scaled here to a school year.
- **Tony Holler / Feed the Cats (high school) — the HS guardrails.** Sprint fast often, in low
  doses, fully rested; record, rank, publish times; never condition sprinters with punishment
  volume. If a kid is not getting faster, something changes.

## Non-negotiables

1. High days are Mon / Wed / Fri (or Tue / Thu / Sat in meet weeks). Never two high days in a row.
2. Loading runs 3 weeks build : 1 week deload, all season.
3. Speed work is timed whenever possible (gates, stopwatch, T-APEX). Record everything.
4. Stop rule: if sprint times are 3%+ off the athlete's current standard, or mechanics fall
   apart, the high session ends. Low day instead.
5. Weights live on high days only (stacked stress, full recovery after), except the base
   circuit in GPP.
6. Both peaks use a 10-day taper: last big session 10 days out, then short 95% touches at
   -8, -6, -4, -2 days, primer (RM-05) the day before, race day Saturday.

## The two tapers (Francis 10-day, MVP volume cut)

- **Mini peak (race Sat Feb 20):** last full high session Wed Feb 10 (MAX-01 + SE-01 short).
  Then 95% touches Fri Feb 12, Sun/Mon Feb 14-15, Wed Feb 17; RM-05 primer Fri Feb 19; race Sat.
  Volume down ~30% across the 10 days, intensity stays at 95%+.
- **Major peak (race Sat May 15):** last full high session Wed May 5 (MAX-01 + SE-03).
  Touches Fri May 7, Sun/Mon May 9-10, Wed May 12; RM-05 primer Fri May 14; race Sat.
  Volume down 40-50% in the final week; this is the one true peak of the year, so the
  February peak is deliberately a "mini" (shorter unload, back to work Feb 22).

## Testing checkpoints

- Wk 4 (Nov 16-22): 30m block time, flying 10/30, countermovement jump, med ball throw.
- Wk 12 (Jan 11-17): flying 30 + first full 60m/200m indoor marks.
- Wk 22 (Mar 22-28): flying 30, 150m time trial.
- Wk 26 (Apr 19-25): 100m/200m season marks vs goals; set championship race plans.
Log results in the workout database as real sessions (v1 of the database roadmap).
